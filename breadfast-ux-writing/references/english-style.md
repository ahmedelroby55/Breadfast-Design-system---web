## 1. Voice & tone

**Personality:** Empathetic, Confident, Ambitious, Innovative, Humble, Accountable. We admit mistakes, never claim to be the best, always aim to be better.

**Tone attributes:**
- **Simple & concise** — clear message, no over-complication, especially in core UX (errors, pop-ups, buttons).
- **Transparent** — honest; own mistakes; never claim perfection.
- **Authentic** — original, differentiated.
- **Approachable** — confident but never arrogant; easy to talk to.
- **Friendly** — a life app, a household essential; can be fun and engaging.

**Summary rules:** concise and direct language; organized and easily understandable (bullets, to the point, not overly sympathetic); active voice, no slang/jargon; friendly, honest, transparent — don't overuse "please"; write positively.

## 2. Tone by message type

- **Success** (order confirmed, delivery arrived) → warm & celebratory. "Your order is confirmed! We're getting it ready for you."
- **Error** (payment failed, out of stock) → helpful & reassuring, **not apologetic**; focus on the solution. "Please select a payment method" style — state the fix, not "Sorry, we couldn't…".
- **Empty states** → encouraging & action-oriented. "Your cart is empty. Ready to shop?" not "You have no items".
- **Promotions** → exciting but not pushy; no FOMO. "New items just dropped!" not "HURRY! LIMITED TIME!!!".

**Serious isn't formal.** Use casual counterparts: Assistance→Help, Commence→Start, Enable→Let, Ensure→Make sure, However→But, In order to→To, Obtain→Get, Provide→Give, Require→Need, Resolve→Fix, Therefore→So, Utilize→Use.

## 3. Grammar & style

### Active vs passive
Use **active** for errors/warnings (clear, actionable, no blame), success (completion), pop-ups/notifications (concise).
- ✅ Enter a valid email address. ❌ A valid email address must be entered.
- ✅ Payment completed successfully. ❌ Your payment has been completed.

Use **passive** to soften/avoid blame, when the actor is unknown or unimportant, or for legal/policy.
- ✅ Your account has been temporarily suspended. (not "We suspended your account")
- ✅ A verification email has been sent. ✅ Refunds will be processed within 5-7 days.
- ✅ The coupon is only valid with {bank_name} cards. (not "You can only use this coupon with…")

### Tense / person
Use "you/your" to address users directly. Third person ("the user") only when referring to someone else.

### Capitalization
**Sentence style everywhere** — titles, subtitles, headlines, buttons, placeholders, tooltips. Don't capitalize random mid-sentence words. Only capitalize pronouns or Breadfast brand names (Breadfast Card, Brekkie by Breadfast, Pay).

### Contractions
Preferred to save space and sound conversational (You will → You'll). Avoid overuse in bank-related content (keep clarity/professionalism).

### Numbers
- Numerals in error messages and where space is tight.
- Spell out in common expressions ("third-party", "all-in-one") and at the start of a sentence.
- A number <10 may be spelled out if no number >9 appears in the same copy.
- Commas over 3 digits: 999, 1,000, 2,000, 15,000, 150,000.

### Time
Numerals + AM/PM in capitals, space between. Hyphen for a period. "Arrived on 20 Dec 2024, 07:52 PM" · "Arrive by 23 Jan 2023, 11:00 AM - 12:00 PM".

### Dates
Day-month-year; abbreviate month and weekday to three letters, no period. "31 Dec 2025" · "Arrived on 23 Jan 2023, 08:46 PM" · "Thu 15 Aug, 05:47 PM".

### Percentages / temperature / ranges
`%` symbol (not "percent"). Degree symbol + capital C ("Keep refrigerated between 3-4°C"). Hyphen for ranges ("It takes 2-3 days").

### Money
Abbreviated currency codes (EGP, USD), not symbols; code **before** the amount in English. "EGP 100 cashback on EGP 600 or more".

### Punctuation
- **Commas:** serial/Oxford comma in lists. "groceries, pharmaceuticals, and pet supplies".
- **Dashes:** hyphen (no spaces) to link words/ranges (first-time user, Monday-Friday); em dash (no spaces) for extra info/emphasis.
- **Periods:** end full sentences with a period; use them in notifications, confirmations, instructional messages. **No period** on titles/headlines, short action-driven messages ("Order delivered", "Tap to continue"), placeholders, or button labels.
- **Question marks:** for direct questions / prompts ("Need help with your order?").
- **Exclamation points:** sparingly, max one; for good news/excitement only — never in neutral/instructional messages or failures/alerts.
- **Semicolons:** sparingly — prefer an em dash or a new sentence. **Colons:** to introduce lists/examples/key info.
- **Ampersands:** in menus, titles, buttons, short phrases (Terms & Conditions, Refer & Earn). Spell out "and" in body text.

## 4. Word choices

### Greetings
Emails: "Hi [First Name]" — use the real name; not "Dear Customer". In-app: "Hi!"/"Hey there!" for casual announcements; start with the message directly for transactional content. Use greetings for announcements/promos, skip for errors and confirmations.

### Addressing customers
"You/your"; "[First Name]" when personalized. Not "Customers" (distant) or "Users" (technical).

### Politeness
- **Skip "please"** in instructions. "Select your payment method" not "Please select…".
- **Helpful, not apologetic.** "This phone number is registered. Try a different number." not "Sorry, this number is already in use".

### Exclamation marks
Only for truly exciting moments (feature launches, offers, delivery arrival). Not for regular confirmations, errors, or instructions.

### Cultural sensitivity
Avoid culturally specific references; simple, consistent writing for translation; consider varying reading levels.

## 5. Component rules

### Titles / headlines / headers
- Make the main point **clear** — sentence case, not title case.
- **Start with an action verb**, not a noun ("Cancel order" not "Order cancellation").
- **Specific**, not generic.
- **Avoid articles** (a/an/the) unless essential.
- **Prioritize key info first** — action first, then context.
- **Don't phrase as a question.** No end period.

### Buttons (CTA)
- Start with a strong **action verb** (Pay, Send, Confirm).
- **Short** — 1-2 words ideal, up to 3-4 if needed ("Back to Pay", "Get your card now").
- **Sentence case** (only first word capitalized unless a Breadfast term).
- **Clear on its own** — reading only the button tells you what happens next.
- May reuse the title's words. **No first person** (I/me/my).

### Placeholders
Brief; sentence case; **no full stops** (some become headlines).

### Messages
Avoid jargon and "please"; clear, informative, concise; don't repeat info; don't capitalize mid-sentence words (unless Breadfast term); **end with a full stop**.

### Error / success messages
Brief to save space. If space is very limited, omit "the"/"Your". Avoid "have been" — use "was" ("The {object} was {verb} successfully."). Full sentences; end with a full stop or split into two short sentences instead of a comma. Avoid jargon and "please".

### Bottom sheets / pop-ups / modals
- Users scan: **Headline → CTA → Body.**
- Put the **primary action** in the headline *and* the CTA.
- Keep subtitles short; buttons use actionable verbs.
- Titles state the main point — **not a question.**
- Don't duplicate the headline and CTA word-for-word; the CTA reinforces the action concisely.

### Empty pages
- **Titles:** concise, sentence case — "No {objects} yet" / "No {object} {past tense} yet".
- **Message:** brief; start with an action verb instead of "You can…".
- **Button:** add a CTA to prompt action if any.

### Tooltips
Short (under ~95 characters) — clarity from getting to the point.

### Marketing (creative language)
For marketing content it's encouraged to be expressive even if longer. "**Share the joy** by recommending your loved ones, and we'll send them free gifts!" / "…**completely on us**!".
